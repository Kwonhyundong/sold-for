I collected 198 real sale prices of software businesses — here's what the data says

────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
BODY
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

Every marketplace tells you what sellers ask. Almost nobody tells you what
buyers actually paid. I went looking for that number, could not find it in one
place, and ended up building the table myself.

https://kwonhyundong.github.io/sold-for/

198 closed deals so far, ranging from $1,000 to $2B. The sources are
marketplace exit prices, broker closed-deal records, and founder posts where
someone named a figure, which I read one by one.

Three findings that changed how I think about pricing a small product:

1. Small products clear at high multiples. Under $10k, the median deal went
   for about 8x annual revenue. In the $100k-$1M band it is about 0.9x. Price
   seems to have a floor near $1,000 whatever the product earns, so a tiny
   product clears at many times its revenue while a real business clears at
   roughly one year of it.

2. Products with almost no revenue still sell. Seven of them, earning under
   $600/year, went for $1,000, $1,000, $1,000, $1,050, $2,000, $5,000 and
   $5,000. If you have something small you stopped working on, that is roughly
   the floor.

3. Web products clear far better than mobile apps. An iOS app with $1,384 of
   annual revenue sold for $1,000. A web platform with $250 sold for $2,000.
   Apple account transfers and review risk appear to cost real money.

I also built a valuation page that takes your revenue and shows the closest
real deals rather than applying a formula, because a formula silently picks a
band and can be wrong about which one:
https://kwonhyundong.github.io/sold-for/worth/

What it cannot tell you: the under-$10k band has only 10 deals with revenue
attached, so treat that 8x as a hint. Broker deals pull the dataset toward
$100k+. And comparables mix SaaS, content sites and ecommerce, which sell at
very different multiples — which is why the individual deals are listed.

Free, no signup, no ads. It re-collects and republishes itself daily.

If you have sold something and are willing to say what it went for, or you
know a closed-deal source I have missed, I would like to add it.
